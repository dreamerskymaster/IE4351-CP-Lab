📋 Introduction

Welcome to the Mechanical and Industrial Engineering (MIE) Department labs, shops, and makerspaces! These spaces are equipped with professional-grade equipment and tools that allow you to bring your engineering designs to life.

🚨 CRITICAL REQUIREMENT

ALL students MUST review this Safety Sheet before using any MIE laboratory, shop, or makerspace.

This is NOT optional. Your safety and the safety of others depends on everyone following these guidelines.

Our Commitment to Your Safety

The MIE Department is committed to providing you with:

  • Safe work environments: All equipment is maintained to the highest standards
  • Proper training: Comprehensive instruction before using any equipment
  • Expert supervision: Experienced lab technicians and teaching assistants
  • Emergency preparedness: Clear procedures and accessible safety equipment
  • Support: Resources and guidance whenever you need them

These safety rules apply to ALL MIE facilities, including:

📜 General Guidelines & Procedures

Before You Start Any Lab Work

1. Know Your Exits

When you first enter ANY lab or shop:

  • Locate all exit doors
  • Identify the fastest route out
  • Note where fire extinguishers are located
  • Find the eye wash stations and safety showers (if present)

2. Know Your Staff

Identify who is responsible for the space:

  • Lab technician(s)
  • Teaching assistant(s)
  • Faculty supervisor

These are the people to contact if you have questions or concerns!

3. Report Unsafe Conditions

If you notice anything unsafe:

  • Damaged equipment
  • Spills or hazards
  • Missing safety guards
  • Unusual smells or sounds

Report immediately to lab staff or the Department Safety Officer!

4. Never Work Alone

The "buddy system" is REQUIRED:

  • Always have at least one other person present
  • Your buddy can help in emergencies
  • You can monitor each other's safety
  • Two sets of eyes catch more mistakes

👥 The Buddy System - Why It Matters

What is the buddy system?

Two people work together as a team, watching out for each other's safety. One person is never alone in the lab.

Why is it required?

  • Emergency response: If you're injured, your buddy can call for help
  • Accident prevention: Two people catch mistakes one person might miss
  • Equipment operation: Some tasks require two people for safe operation
  • Mental awareness: Working alone leads to complacency and mistakes

What if I'm the only person from my class?

You can work with students from other classes, courses, or groups, as long as someone else is present and aware you're in the lab. They don't need to be doing the same project, just present and able to help if needed.

Reporting Accidents and Incidents

If an accident occurs:
  1. Immediate action:
    • Stop all work immediately
    • Turn off equipment if safe to do so
    • Secure the area
  2. Notify technical staff or TA immediately
  3. If they're not available:
    • Contact your course instructor
    • Contact the Department Safety Officer (see contact info below)
  4. For serious emergencies: Call NUPD at 3333
  5. After the situation is controlled: The Department Safety Officer must be notified for documentation

💡 What Counts as an "Incident"?

Report ALL of these, even if no one is hurt:

  • Any injury, no matter how minor (cuts, burns, pinches)
  • Near-miss accidents (almost got hurt but didn't)
  • Equipment damage or malfunction
  • Chemical spills or exposure
  • Fire or smoke
  • Unsafe behavior that could have caused injury

Why report near-misses? They help identify hazards before someone actually gets hurt!

🥽 Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

⚠️ PPE is REQUIRED, Not Optional

You MUST wear appropriate PPE at all times when in the lab. This is not a suggestion - it's a requirement for your safety!

No PPE = No Lab Access

What is PPE?

Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) is equipment worn to minimize exposure to hazards that cause serious injuries and illnesses. Think of it as your personal armor against workshop dangers.

Required PPE for MIE Labs

👓

Safety Glasses

REQUIRED in all lab areas

Protects eyes from:

  • Flying chips and particles
  • Splashing liquids
  • Sparks
  • Debris

Must be ANSI Z87.1 approved

👂

Hearing Protection

REQUIRED in high-noise areas

Protects hearing from:

  • Loud machinery
  • Pneumatic tools
  • Grinding operations
  • Impact tools

Use earplugs or earmuffs when noise exceeds 85 dB

🧤

Hand Protection

REQUIRED for specific tasks

Protects hands from:

  • Sharp edges and burrs
  • Hot surfaces
  • Chemicals
  • Abrasive materials

⚠️ WARNING: NEVER wear gloves near rotating machinery!

👟

Closed-Toe Shoes

REQUIRED at all times

Protects feet from:

  • Dropped tools or parts
  • Heavy equipment
  • Rolling hazards
  • Chemical spills

NO sandals, flip-flops, or open-toe shoes allowed!

😷

Respiratory Protection

REQUIRED for specific operations

Protects lungs from:

  • Dust particles
  • Chemical fumes
  • Mist or spray
  • Airborne contaminants

Must be fit-tested and approved - ask lab staff

👕

Appropriate Clothing

REQUIRED for machine work

Requirements:

  • NO loose clothing
  • NO jewelry or watches
  • Tie back long hair
  • Short or fitted sleeves
  • Long pants (no shorts)

Loose items can get caught in machinery!

Proper Machine Shop Attire

⚠️ You Will Be Turned Away If Improperly Dressed

The lab technician or TA has the authority to refuse lab access to anyone not wearing proper attire. This is for YOUR protection!

  • Fitted long-sleeve shirt (cuffs buttoned) + work pants + boots + safety glasses
  • Lab coat over regular clothes + long pants + closed-toe shoes + safety glasses

  • Tank top, loose hoodie, baggy jacket
  • Shorts, skirt, dress
  • Sandals, flip-flops, Crocs, open-toe shoes
  • Dangling necklaces, large earrings, rings, watches
  • Long loose hair not tied back

If you show up like this, you will NOT be allowed to work in the lab!

📏 Specific Lab Rules

Equipment Operation Rules

🚨 RULE #1: Never Operate Without Training

The technical staff and TAs have the authority to refuse use of equipment to anyone who:

  • Has not been properly trained on the equipment
  • Does not follow safe operating procedures
  • Refuses to accept instruction
  • Demonstrates unsafe behavior

This will be reported to your course instructor and could affect your grade.

🔧 Before Using Any Equipment

  • Verify you've been trained on this specific equipment
  • Review the Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) if available
  • Inspect the equipment for damage or issues
  • Ensure all safety guards are in place
  • Know where the emergency stop is located

⚠️ If Equipment Malfunctions

  • DO NOT attempt to repair it yourself
  • Turn it off and unplug if safe to do so
  • Tag it as "OUT OF SERVICE"
  • Report to lab technician or TA immediately
  • Use different equipment if available

🤝 If You Need Help

  • Never be afraid to ask questions!
  • If you feel uncomfortable, STOP and ask for assistance
  • It's better to ask a "stupid question" than have a serious accident
  • Lab staff would rather answer 100 questions than treat one injury

🚫 Prohibited Behaviors

  • Horseplay or roughhousing
  • Running in the lab
  • Distracting others operating equipment
  • Using equipment for unauthorized purposes
  • Bypassing or removing safety features

These will result in immediate removal from the lab!

Food and Beverage Policy

🚫 NO Food or Drinks in Lab Areas

Why this rule exists:

  • Contamination risk: Chemicals or metal particles can get into food/drinks
  • Spill hazards: Liquids can damage equipment or create slip hazards
  • Distraction: Eating/drinking takes attention away from dangerous tasks
  • Hygiene: Your hands may have picked up contaminants from equipment

Designated eating areas:

  • Break rooms or lounges (clearly marked)
  • Outside the lab building
  • Student common areas

If you need water: Take a break, leave the lab area, wash your hands first, then drink from the fountain or your water bottle in a designated area.

After-Hours and Unsupervised Access

📅 NU Policy 602: Restricted Access

Some equipment and spaces have restricted access hours when direct supervision is not available.

What this means:

  • Certain high-risk equipment may only be used when a technician is present
  • Some labs close after regular hours
  • Even if the building is open, specific rooms may be locked
  • 24/7 access may require special permission and safety training

Check with your lab technician or instructor about access hours for your specific lab.

Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)

💡 What are SOPs?

Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) are detailed, written instructions for safely operating specific equipment or conducting specific processes.

SOPs are REQUIRED for:

  • Student group activities involving chemicals
  • Use of hazardous materials or equipment
  • Processes with environmental or exposure concerns
  • Any activity that could present safety risks

SOPs must be:

  • Written before starting the activity
  • Reviewed by your faculty advisor
  • Approved by the Department Safety Officer
  • Posted in the work area
  • Followed exactly

Working with Hazardous Materials

⚠️ Special Procedures Required

If your project involves any of these, you MUST develop an SOP and get approval:

  • Chemicals (solvents, acids, bases, etc.)
  • Compressed gases
  • High temperatures (ovens, furnaces, welding)
  • High voltages
  • Lasers (Class 3B or Class 4)
  • Cryogenic materials (liquid nitrogen, dry ice)
  • Nanomaterials
  • Biological materials

🚨 Emergency Procedures

Types of Emergencies and Responses

🔥 Fire Emergency

If you discover a fire:
  1. Immediately activate the fire alarm (pull station)
  2. Alert everyone in the area: Shout "FIRE!" clearly
  3. Evacuate the building:
    • Use the nearest safe exit
    • Do NOT use elevators
    • Help others if you can do so safely
    • Close doors behind you (don't lock them)
  4. Call NUPD from a safe location: Dial 3333 or 617-373-3333
  5. Do NOT re-enter until fire department says it's safe

Small Fires (wastebasket size or smaller):

Only if you've been trained and feel safe doing so:

  1. Use the appropriate fire extinguisher (see PASS method below)
  2. If fire doesn't go out immediately, evacuate and call NUPD
  3. Never fight a fire that's spreading or blocking your exit

PASS Method for Fire Extinguishers:

  • Pull the pin
  • Aim at the base of the fire
  • Squeeze the handle
  • Sweep side to side

🩹 Medical Emergency / Injury

If someone is injured:
  1. Ensure the area is safe:
    • Turn off equipment if it's still running
    • Make sure there's no continuing danger
  2. Call for help immediately:
    • Shout for the lab technician or TA
    • If serious: Call NUPD at 3333
  3. Provide first aid if trained:
    • Don't move the injured person unless necessary
    • Apply pressure to stop bleeding
    • Keep the person calm and still
  4. Do NOT provide medical care beyond your training level
  5. Stay with the injured person until help arrives

🏥 First Aid Locations

  • First aid kits are located in each lab (ask staff where)
  • Eye wash stations in labs with chemical hazards
  • Safety showers in designated areas
  • AED (defibrillator) locations marked in buildings

🧪 Chemical Spill or Exposure

If chemicals are spilled:
  1. Alert everyone nearby
  2. Evacuate the immediate area if fumes are present
  3. For small spills (you can see the edges):
    • Notify lab staff
    • Use spill kit if you've been trained
    • Follow SOP for that chemical
  4. For large spills or unknown chemicals:
    • Evacuate the area
    • Call NUPD at 3333
    • Provide Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS) if available
If chemicals contact your skin or eyes:
  1. Eyes:
    • Go to eye wash station IMMEDIATELY
    • Flush eyes for at least 15 minutes
    • Hold eyelids open while flushing
    • Have someone call NUPD
  2. Skin:
    • Remove contaminated clothing
    • Use safety shower if large area affected
    • Flush with water for at least 15 minutes
    • Seek medical attention

⚡ Electrical Emergency

If someone is being shocked:
  1. DO NOT TOUCH THEM - you could be shocked too!
  2. Cut the power:
    • Turn off the equipment at its switch
    • Unplug it if you can do so safely
    • Or flip the circuit breaker
  3. Call NUPD at 3333
  4. Only after power is off: Check if person is breathing, provide CPR if trained

⚠️ Electrical Safety Rules

  • Never work on electrical equipment with wet hands
  • Don't bypass or remove grounding pins
  • Report frayed cords or damaged plugs immediately
  • Don't overload outlets
  • Keep electrical panels accessible (don't block with equipment)

✅ Good Laboratory Practices

General Safety Mindset

🧠 Think Safety First

Developing a safety mindset means:

  • Always ask "What could go wrong?" before starting any task
  • Plan your work: Think through each step before acting
  • Stay alert: Fatigue and distraction cause accidents
  • Learn from mistakes: If something goes wrong, understand why
  • Speak up: If you see unsafe behavior, say something
  • Don't rush: Taking your time prevents mistakes

Housekeeping and Cleanliness

A clean lab is a safe lab! Poor housekeeping causes:

  • Slip and trip hazards: From debris, spills, or cluttered walkways
  • Fire hazards: From accumulated dust, oily rags, or scrap materials
  • Contamination: Mixing incompatible materials
  • Lost tools: Wasting time searching for misplaced equipment
  • Poor quality work: Dirt and chips affecting precision

✅ Clean Workspace Habits

  • Clean as you go: Don't wait until the end of your session
  • Sweep regularly: Remove chips and debris frequently
  • Wipe up spills immediately: Oil, coolant, or water creates slip hazards
  • Return tools to proper storage: Don't leave them on benches
  • Dispose of waste properly: Use designated containers for different materials
  • Keep walkways clear: Don't block aisles or exits
  • Leave it cleaner than you found it: Be a good lab citizen!

Situational Awareness

💡 The 360° Awareness Rule

Always be aware of:

  • What YOU are doing: Focus on your task
  • What's around you: People, equipment, hazards
  • What others are doing: Could affect your safety
  • Exit routes: Can you get out quickly if needed?
  • Equipment state: What's on, what's off, what's moving

Examples of good awareness:

  • Looking around before moving a large part
  • Noticing someone about to turn on equipment near you
  • Hearing a machine making unusual sounds
  • Seeing a spill before someone slips on it

Incident Reporting

📝 ALL Incidents Must Be Reported

Even if no one was hurt, report:

  • Near-miss accidents
  • Equipment malfunctions
  • Safety violations you witnessed
  • Unsafe conditions
  • Minor injuries (cuts, bruises, burns)

Why reporting matters:

  • Helps identify and fix hazards before serious accidents occur
  • Creates a record for insurance and liability
  • Allows department to improve safety procedures
  • You won't get in trouble for reporting - we want to know!

🎯 Common Safety Scenarios

Scenario-Based Learning

Let's walk through some common situations and the correct responses:

Scenario 1: You Cut Your Hand on a Sharp Burr

Situation: While handling a freshly machined part, you cut your finger on a sharp edge. It's bleeding but not severely.

Correct Response:
  1. Stop what you're doing immediately
  2. Apply pressure with a clean cloth or paper towel
  3. Notify the lab technician or TA
  4. Wash the wound with soap and water
  5. Apply bandage from first aid kit
  6. Fill out incident report (even for minor cuts!)
  7. If cut is deep or won't stop bleeding: Call NUPD at 3333

✅ Prevention:

  • Always deburr parts after machining
  • Use a file or deburring tool on sharp edges
  • Wear cut-resistant gloves when handling sharp parts
  • Handle parts carefully, don't grab quickly

Scenario 2: The CNC Machine Makes a Strange Grinding Sound

Situation: You're running a CNC program and suddenly hear a loud grinding or squealing noise that wasn't there before.

Correct Response:
  1. Press the emergency stop button immediately
  2. Do NOT try to diagnose the problem yourself
  3. Notify the lab technician or TA right away
  4. Document what you were doing when it happened
  5. Wait for technician to inspect before resuming

❌ Wrong Response:

  • Continuing to run the machine ("maybe it will stop")
  • Opening the door while it's still moving
  • Trying to fix it yourself
  • Not reporting it because you're worried about getting in trouble

Scenario 3: You Smell Something Burning Near the Robot

Situation: While the robot is running your program, you notice a burning smell coming from the control cabinet.

Correct Response:
  1. Stop the robot immediately (emergency stop or pause button)
  2. Alert everyone in the area
  3. Notify lab staff immediately
  4. If you see smoke or flames:
    • Activate fire alarm
    • Evacuate the area
    • Call NUPD at 3333
  5. Do NOT attempt to open the control cabinet

Scenario 4: Another Student is Not Following Safety Rules

Situation: You notice another student operating a machine without safety glasses, or with loose clothing that could get caught.

Correct Response:
  1. Politely remind them of the safety requirement
  2. If they ignore you or argue: Notify the lab technician or TA immediately
  3. Do NOT let them pressure you into being silent
  4. You have a responsibility to help keep everyone safe

✅ How to Handle It:

Say something like: "Hey, I noticed you don't have safety glasses on. The lab requires them at all times. There are extras by the door if you need a pair."

Most people will appreciate the reminder! Safety is everyone's responsibility.

Scenario 5: You're Not Sure How to Use a Tool Safely

Situation: Your project requires using a tool you've never used before, and you're not confident about the safe way to operate it.

Correct Response:
  1. DO NOT attempt to use it without training
  2. Ask the lab technician or TA for instruction
  3. Request a demonstration
  4. Practice under supervision
  5. Ask questions until you feel confident

✅ Remember:

There are NO stupid questions when it comes to safety!

Lab staff would much rather spend 10 minutes teaching you than dealing with an accident. Never feel embarrassed to ask for help.

🏭 Lab-Specific Safety Rules

CNC Machining Lab

Before Operating

  • Verify safety guards are in place
  • Check that workpiece is secured
  • Ensure tools are properly installed
  • Close and lock the machine door

During Operation

  • Never open door while spindle is rotating
  • Stay at the machine (don't leave it running unattended)
  • Keep hands away from rotating tools
  • Use chip hook to remove chips, never your hands

After Operation

  • Wait for spindle to stop completely
  • Remove chips with brush or chip hook
  • Clean the machine table
  • Return tools to proper storage

Robotics Lab

Before Operating

  • Verify the work area is clear
  • Test emergency stop buttons
  • Ensure safety fencing/barriers are in place
  • Start programs at reduced speed (10-25%)

During Operation

  • NEVER enter the robot's work envelope while running
  • Stay alert - robots can move suddenly
  • Keep hand near emergency stop
  • Watch for unexpected movements

Teaching Mode Safety

  • Only authorized personnel can teach robots
  • Use deadman switch (teach pendant enable button)
  • Move at reduced speed
  • Be aware of pinch points

Makerspace (3D Printing, Laser Cutting, etc.)

3D Printing Safety

  • Never touch hot end or heated bed (can cause severe burns)
  • Ensure adequate ventilation when printing
  • Don't leave prints running unattended for long periods
  • Let parts cool before removing from bed
  • Clean build plate after each print

Laser Cutter Safety

  • Never bypass door interlocks
  • Only cut approved materials (NO PVC, vinyl, etc.)
  • Ensure exhaust fan is running
  • NEVER leave laser cutter unattended while cutting
  • Have fire extinguisher nearby

Hand Tools Safety

  • Use the right tool for the job
  • Inspect tools before use (damaged tools = danger)
  • Secure workpiece before cutting, drilling, etc.
  • Cut away from your body
  • Return tools to storage when done

⭐ Safety Best Practices

The Safety Hierarchy

🎯 Hierarchy of Controls (Most Effective to Least)

  1. Elimination: Remove the hazard entirely (best option!)
    • Example: Use a safer chemical instead of a toxic one
  2. Substitution: Replace with something less hazardous
    • Example: Use water-based coolant instead of oil-based
  3. Engineering Controls: Isolate people from hazards
    • Example: Safety guards on machines, ventilation systems
  4. Administrative Controls: Change how people work
    • Example: Training programs, SOPs, warning signs
  5. PPE: Protect the individual (last line of defense)
    • Example: Safety glasses, gloves, hearing protection

Note: PPE is important but it's the LAST line of defense. The best safety comes from eliminating hazards or using engineering controls!

Pre-Task Planning

✅ Before Starting ANY Task, Ask Yourself:

  1. What am I trying to accomplish? (Clear goal)
  2. What could go wrong? (Identify hazards)
  3. What's the worst that could happen? (Assess risk)
  4. How can I prevent it? (Plan controls)
  5. What will I do if something goes wrong? (Emergency plan)
  6. Do I have the right training and PPE? (Verify readiness)

Taking 2 minutes to think through these questions can prevent hours of problems!

Communication

📢 Effective Lab Communication

  • Before starting equipment: Alert people nearby ("I'm starting the mill")
  • When moving large objects: Warn others in your path
  • If you see a hazard: Speak up immediately
  • When leaving your station: Let your buddy know
  • If you need help: Ask clearly and specifically

Stress and Fatigue Management

⚠️ Tired = More Accidents

Recognize when you're not at your best:

  • Fatigue: Been working for hours without a break
  • Stress: Worried about deadlines, exams, personal issues
  • Distraction: Mind is elsewhere, not focused on the task
  • Illness: Not feeling well, taking medication that affects alertness

If you're not at 100%:

  • Take a break
  • Do less critical tasks (documentation, planning, cleanup)
  • Come back when you're more alert
  • It's okay to say "I'm too tired to safely operate this equipment right now"

Chemical spill (small) Alert lab staff, use spill kit if trained Lab tech/TA
Electrical shock Cut power, don't touch victim until power is off NUPD: 3333
Equipment malfunction Emergency stop, turn off power Lab tech/TA
Someone trapped in equipment Emergency stop, call for help, DON'T try to free them yourself NUPD: 3333
Unsafe condition (no immediate danger) Mark/cordon off area, alert others Lab tech/TA, then Safety Officer
Feeling unwell or dizzy Stop working, sit down, get fresh air Lab tech/TA, then Health Services if needed

Safety Equipment Locations

📍 Know Where These Are Located

In or near every lab, you should know the location of:

  • Fire extinguishers: Usually near exits or in hallways
  • Fire alarm pull stations: Near exits
  • Emergency stop buttons: On all machinery
  • First aid kits: In the lab or with lab staff
  • Eye wash stations: In labs with chemicals (marked with green signs)
  • Safety showers: In labs with chemicals
  • Spill kits: Near chemical storage areas
  • Emergency exits: All exit routes from the room
  • AED (defibrillator): In building hallways (marked with red/white signs)
  • Emergency phone: Or know that your cell phone works in the lab

⚖️ Your Rights and Responsibilities

Your Rights as a Student

✅ You Have the Right To:

  • A safe work environment: Free from recognized hazards
  • Proper training: Before using any equipment
  • Appropriate PPE: Provided by the lab at no cost to you
  • Refuse unsafe work: If you believe something is dangerous, you can say no
  • Report safety concerns: Without fear of retaliation
  • Access to SOPs and safety information: For all equipment you use
  • Ask questions: No matter how basic they seem

Your Responsibilities as a Student

📋 You Are Responsible For:

  • Following all safety rules and procedures
  • Wearing appropriate PPE at all times
  • Completing required safety training
  • Operating equipment only after proper training
  • Reporting hazards and incidents immediately
  • Keeping your work area clean and organized
  • Looking out for the safety of others
  • Following instructions from lab staff
  • Not working alone (buddy system)
  • Asking for help when unsure

Consequences of Safety Violations

⚠️ Serious Safety Violations Will Result In:

  1. Immediate removal from the lab for that session
  2. Report to course instructor (may affect your grade)
  3. Temporary or permanent ban from lab facilities
  4. Required additional safety training before being allowed back
  5. Disciplinary action through the university if severe

Examples of serious violations:

  • Operating equipment without training or authorization
  • Bypassing or disabling safety features
  • Ignoring direct safety instructions from staff
  • Putting others at risk through reckless behavior
  • Using equipment while impaired

✅ Final Notes

Safety is Everyone's Responsibility

Creating a safe lab environment requires participation from everyone:

  • Faculty and staff: Provide training, maintain equipment, enforce rules
  • You (students): Follow procedures, report hazards, help each other
  • The university: Provide resources, oversight, emergency response

🤝 We're All in This Together

Safety is not about rules for the sake of rules. It's about making sure everyone goes home healthy at the end of the day.

  • Look out for each other
  • Speak up when you see something unsafe
  • Help new students learn the rules
  • Lead by example
